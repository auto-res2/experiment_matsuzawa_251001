# Experimental Design
## Experiment Strategy
Objective: Build a single, coherent validation pipeline that proves DyGraM-HPO is (a) faster, (b) at least as accurate, and (c) more broadly useful than fixed-subset or full-data HPO, while remaining stable to design-choice and task variations.

1. Key Properties to Validate
   1.1 Performance-Accuracy Trade-off – wall-clock speed-up vs. best-achieved test metric.
   1.2 Ranking Fidelity – correlation between early (subset) and final (full-data) HP ranking.
   1.3 Adaptivity Benefit – how subset sizes evolve and how often expansions occur.
   1.4 Computational Efficiency – GPU hours, FLOPs, memory and communication overhead.
   1.5 Robustness & Generalization – stability over seeds, tasks, model scales, HP searchers, and τ / K schedules.

2. Comparison Grid (applied in every experiment)
   • Strong baselines: FULL (no sub-sampling), RANDOM-p% (same fraction), and AUTOMATA-p% (best fixed fraction per prior work).
   • State-of-the-art adaptive subset/HPO methods if available (e.g., HyperShrink, SHA++).
   • Internal ablations:
       – DyGraM-HPO-noDecay (fixed τ)
       – DyGraM-HPO-noCache (recompute g_full each epoch) to isolate caching benefit
       – DyGraM-HPO-fixedStart (start at 10%) to isolate starting-size choice.

3. Validation Angles
   A. Quantitative Metrics
      • Speed-up = GPU-time(FULL)/GPU-time(method) @ equal final accuracy.
      • Accuracy Gap = |Acc(method) – Acc(FULL)|.
      • Ranking Spearman ρ between top-k configs under subset vs. full evaluation.
      • Sample Efficiency = best-valid-score vs. wall-clock curve (any-time plot).
      • Resource Usage = FLOPs, peak VRAM, number of gradient-similarity tests.
   B. Qualitative / Diagnostic
      • Subset-size trajectory per configuration.
      • Δ_t (cosine distance) histogram over epochs.
      • Survival curves of configurations under ASHA (did noise kill good ones?).
      • Heat-maps of τ, K sensitivity.

4. Multi-Perspective Demonstration Plan
   Phase 1: Reproduce baseline numbers on CIFAR-10/100, SST-2, LIBSVM to establish trust.
   Phase 2: Controlled speed/accuracy studies under identical HP-search budgets (50 trials, same seeds) comparing all baselines + DyGraM variants; record above metrics.
   Phase 3: Stress-tests
       – Larger backbone (ResNet-50) and larger dataset (ImageNet-64 subset) to test scaling.
       – Different searchers (Random+ASHA, TPE+ASHA, BOHB) to show searcher-agnosticism.
       – Perturbed τ/K schedules and noise-injected gradients to test robustness.
   Phase 4: Ablation synthesis – compile results to quantify contribution of each design element.

5. Success Criteria (must satisfy ALL in ≥80% of tasks/seeds)
   • Speed-up ≥1.5× over best fixed AUTOMATA and ≥6× over FULL.
   • Accuracy Gap ≤0.5% absolute (classification) or within 95% CI of FULL.
   • Spearman ρ ≥0.9 for top-20% configurations after pruning.
   • Additional gradient-similarity overhead ≤5% of total runtime.
   • Subset expansions per config ≤log2(1/fraction_min) +1 (empirically ≤4).
   • No catastrophic failures: worst-case gap <2% on any task/seed.

6. Practical Execution Notes
   • Use NVIDIA A100 nodes; run jobs in containers with deterministic cudnn settings; cap GPU util ≥90% to reflect realistic high-throughput workload.
   • Record raw logs and publish scripts for full reproducibility.
   • Employ 3 random seeds per experiment; report mean ± std.

This unified strategy, applied verbatim to every benchmark, ensures that DyGraM-HPO’s claimed benefits are examined from efficiency, accuracy, robustness, and usability viewpoints, against rigorous baselines and ablations, with clear quantitative thresholds defining success.
