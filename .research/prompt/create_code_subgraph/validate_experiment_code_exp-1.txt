# Experimental Design
## Experiment Strategy
Overall Goal
Validate that Dropout-Augmented DNN-MFBO (DO-DNN-MFBO) supplies better-calibrated uncertainty and yields faster / cheaper Bayesian-optimisation progress than existing deterministic-weight surrogates, while adding minimal computational overhead, and that these gains persist across functions, fidelities, budgets and hyper-parameters.

1. Validation Axes (what to prove)
1.1 Optimisation efficiency – lower simple (and cumulative) regret per wall-clock time and per monetary/ fidelity cost.
1.2 Uncertainty quality – better calibration and sharper epistemic/ aleatoric separation.
1.3 Robustness – stability to random seeds, MC-sample count, dropout rate, architecture depth/width and noisy observations.
1.4 Generalisation – transfer to unseen objective classes, higher-dimensional domains and real engineering tasks.
1.5 Computational economy – <10 % extra GPU time, negligible extra CPU/RAM, fits in 16 GB VRAM and 500 MB host RAM.

2. Comparative Landscape (with whom to compete)
• Primary baseline: original DNN-MFBO (identical architecture, training, acquisition).
• Classical surrogate: single-fidelity (and multi-fidelity) GP with Matérn-5/2 kernel (gold-standard uncertainty).
• SOTA deterministic DNN surrogates: BOHAMIANN, Deep Ensembles, Batch-Norm-DFBO.
• Internal ablations:
  – Dropout only in final layer (tests ‘Bayesian-everywhere’ claim).
  – Different dropout rates {0.05,0.1,0.2}.
  – Different MC pass counts {5,20,100}.
  – Deterministic inference using μ alone (tests importance of σ²).

3. Experimental Angles (how to assess)
3.1 Quantitative
    a. Optimisation: simple & cumulative regret vs. cost and vs. time.
    b. Calibration: NLL, expected calibration error (ECE), predictive interval coverage (PICP), sharpness.
    c. Robustness: variance of metrics across ≥30 seeds; worst-50-percentile performance.
    d. Cost: average GPU milliseconds per forward-pass; total training + BO loop wall time.
3.2 Qualitative
    • Uncertainty maps on 2-D problems (heat-maps of σ̂²).
    • Acquisition trajectories (plots of queried points coloured by fidelity & time).
    • Example MC sample traces showing epistemic spread shrinkage.
3.3 Statistical Rigour
    • Wilcoxon signed-rank or paired t-tests between methods (α=0.05, Holm correction).
    • 95 % confidence bands on regret curves via bootstrap.

4. Task Suite (where to test)
Synthetic (cheap, controlled)
  • 2-D Branin, Hartmann-6, Ackley-10 with multi-fidelity variants.
  • Noisy (σ²_truth) vs. noise-free setups.
Real / Semi-real (resource aware)
  • Hyper-parameter tuning of a small CNN on CIFAR-10 (proxy trained 3 epochs for low fidelity, full 50 epochs for high).
  • Wing-profile drag simulation with CFD mesh resolution as fidelity.
Tasks are chosen to fit into ≤16 GB GPU and stream data to stay within 500 MB host RAM.

5. Unified Protocol (applied identically across tasks)
Step-0  Initialise identical warm-start design (Latin Hypercube) shared by all contestants.
Step-1  Train surrogate(s) for k_init warm-up steps.
Step-2  BO loop for T iterations or until budget B is exhausted.
Step-3  Re-train surrogates fully every q iterations (q chosen so GPU utilisation ≈80 %).
Step-4  Record metrics after each evaluation.
Step-5  Repeat N_seeds independent runs, aggregate statistics.

6. Success Criteria
• DO-DNN-MFBO achieves statistically significant lower median simple regret (≥15 % relative improvement) at equal cost and time on ≥75 % of tasks.
• ECE and NLL improved by ≥10 % on average; predictive-interval coverage within ±3 % of nominal 95 % level.
• Runtime overhead ≤1.1× baseline; fits given hardware without OOM.
• Gains persist under all ablation settings except those that explicitly remove dropout or MC sampling (validates causal mechanism).

7. Practical Considerations
• Use mixed-precision (fp16) and in-place ops to meet VRAM/RAM caps.
• Cache MC forward passes per batch to reuse GPU kernels.
• Employ deterministic dataloaders to control seed influence.
• All code instrumented with PyTorch profiler for cost metrics.

This unified strategy ensures every experiment—synthetic or real, single- or multi-fidelity—evaluates DO-DNN-MFBO along the same dimensions, against the same opponents, using the same statistical yardsticks, thereby providing a cohesive, multi-perspective demonstration of the new method’s effectiveness.

# Current Experiment (To be validated)
- Experiment ID: exp-1
- Description: Objective / hypothesis: Quantitatively and qualitatively verify that injecting Monte-Carlo dropout in every hidden layer (DO-DNN-MFBO) provides (i) better-calibrated predictive uncertainty and (ii) faster Bayesian-optimisation progress, while adding ≤10 % computational overhead, on a suite of controlled multi-fidelity synthetic tasks.  Secondary goals: assess robustness to dropout rate, check sensitivity to MC sample count, and compare against an established stochastic surrogate (Deep Ensemble).

1. Models
   • orig-DNN-MFBO: 3-layer MLP (128 ReLU units) – deterministic weights, Bayesian last layer only.
   • DO-DNN-MFBO (ours): identical MLP + dropout(p=0.1) after every hidden layer; MC inference with N=20 passes.
   • DO-DNN-MFBO-drop0.05 / drop0.2: same as above with p∈{0.05,0.20} for hyper-param sensitivity.
   • DeepEnsemble-MFBO: 5 independent MLPs, last-layer Bayesian; acquisition built from ensemble mean/variance.
   • Classical GP surrogate (Matérn-5/2) is used only for calibration sanity-check plots but excluded from run_variations to stay within 5 variants.

2. Datasets / Tasks
   a. Two-fidelity Branin-2D (low-fidelity 50×50 grid sample; high-fidelity continuous).  Costs λ=(1,10).
   b. Two-fidelity Hartmann-6 (1 000 / 10 000 latent evaluations).  Costs λ=(1,5).
   c. Ackley-10 (single-fidelity noise-free) for dimensional-scalability sanity.
   All functions stream batched evaluations to keep host-RAM <500 MB.

3. Pre-processing
   • Inputs rescaled to [0,1]^d; outputs standardised per seed using initial Latin-Hypercube warm-start points.
   • Fidelity index appended to input vector for multi-fidelity tasks.

4. Data Splitting / BO protocol
   • Warm-start: 5d random LHS points per fidelity.
   • BO loop: 25 iterations (Branin), 60 (Hartmann-6), 100 (Ackley-10) or until monetary budget B is exhausted (B=150 for Branin, 300 for Hartmann-6).
   • Re-train surrogate every 5 iterations.
   • 30 independent seeds.  Results aggregated as median ±95 % bootstrap CI.

5. Evaluation metrics
   Primary: simple regret vs. consumed cost; area-under-regret-curve (AURC).
   Secondary: cumulative regret, wall-clock time, GPU-hours, NLL, ECE (15-bin), PICP@95 %, sharpness (mean predictive std), FLOPs/iteration, VRAM peak.

6. Selection criteria
   • At each iteration we log both ‘last’ model and ‘best-val-NLL’ checkpoint; final curves shown for best-val-NLL but robustness analysis reports both.

7. Statistical analysis
   • Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test on AURC between DO-DNN-MFBO and every other variant (Holm-corrected α=0.05).
   • Effect size = median relative improvement.

8. Hyper-parameter & robustness analysis
   • Compare drop0.05 vs. drop0.1 vs. drop0.2.
   • Re-run DO-DNN-MFBO with MC-samples N∈{5,20,100} on a 5-seed subset; report ECE and runtime scaling.
   • Add Gaussian noise N(0,0.01) to observed outputs in 10 seeds to test noise robustness.

9. Computational-efficiency logging
   • PyTorch profiler records forward/backward FLOPs, kernel time, memory.  Per-iteration wall-time and VRAM captured via nvidia-smi.
   • Overhead = (time_DO − time_orig)/time_orig.
