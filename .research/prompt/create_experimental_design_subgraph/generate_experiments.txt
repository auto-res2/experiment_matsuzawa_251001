# Experiment Strategy
Objective
Demonstrate, in a principled and statistically-sound manner, that replacing BOIL’s learnt-sigmoid compression with the proposed parameter-free AAUC compression (AAUC-BOIL) leads to (i) equal-or-better optimisation quality, (ii) lower computational overhead, (iii) improved robustness to noise/curve length, and (iv) wider task-level generalisation – all on hardware no stronger than a single NVIDIA-T4 with 16 GB VRAM and 0.5 GB system RAM.

Core Hypotheses to Validate
1. Effectiveness – AAUC-BOIL finds better (or at worst equal) objective values within the same Bayesian-optimisation (BO) budget.
2. Efficiency – AAUC-BOIL reduces per-iteration wall-clock time and GP hyper-parameter optimisation time by ≥20 %.
3. Robustness – AAUC-BOIL is less sensitive to
   a. random seeds,
   b. noisy/short curves, and
   c. miss-scaled rewards, than sigmoid-BOIL.
4. Generalisation – performance and efficiency gains hold across algorithm families, data domains, and curve lengths without task-specific tuning.

Common Experimental Axes
A. Quantitative optimisation quality: best-so-far validation/test score after a fixed BO budget; area-under-best-curve (AUBC) across iterations.
B. Computational cost: (i) wall-clock per BO step, (ii) total wall-clock until budget exhaustion, (iii) GPU utilisation %, (iv) peak RAM/VRAM.
C. Numerical stability: GP kernel log-condition-number, number of Cholesky failures / jitter injections.
D. Robustness: standard deviation and inter-quartile range of the above over ≥10 seeds; performance degradation when Gaussian noise or early truncation is injected in the learning curves.
E. Ablation sensitivity: effect of discount factor α, weight schedule w_t, and alternative simple compressions (e.g. last-value, raw AUC) to isolate contributions.

Comparative Baselines (applied in every experiment)
1. Sigmoid-BOIL (original implementation) – primary baseline.
2. Pure random search – sanity check.
3. State-of-the-art curve-aware BO methods without learnable compression (e.g. FABOLAS, Hyperband) – to situate AAUC-BOIL versus broader literature.
4. Ablations of AAUC-BOIL:
   a. No discount (w_t ≡1),
   b. Uniform α values (α∈{1,2,4,8}),
   c. AAUC with rescaled inputs (to test scale-invariance).

Task Suite (scalable to the hardware envelope)
1. Vision: MNIST and CIFAR-10 classification (small CNN/MLP).
2. NLP: Penn Treebank word-level language model (2-layer LSTM).
3. Reinforcement Learning: CartPole-v1 policy-gradient agent (short/noisy curves).
4. Tabular: UCI regression datasets with XGBoost (fewer iterations, different curve shapes).
All tasks capped so that a single training run fits ≤1 GB VRAM and ≤400 MB RAM; batching and checkpointing used where necessary.

Experimental Protocol (identical for every task)
1. Fixed BO budget (e.g. 20 iterations) with identical Sobol initial design.
2. 10–20 independent random seeds.
3. Record metrics A–C at every BO iteration; compute robustness statistics post-hoc.
4. Inject two stress settings per task:
   • Additive Gaussian noise (σ chosen to give ≈5 % accuracy SD).
   • Early-stop curves after 30 % and 60 % of nominal length.
5. Run all methods under identical time limits; abort runs that exceed 16 GB VRAM or 500 MB RAM.

Success/Validation Criteria
• Effectiveness: ≥1 % relative improvement in final score OR statistically non-inferior (paired t-test p>0.05) while meeting efficiency goal.
• Efficiency: ≥20 % mean reduction in per-iteration wall-clock time.
• Robustness: ≥30 % reduction in seed-to-seed variance OR ≤5 % performance loss under noise/truncation relative to clean setting.
• Generalisation: above criteria satisfied on ≥3 of 4 task families.
If all are met, AAUC-BOIL is deemed superior.

Multi-Perspective Demonstration
1. Aggregate quantitative tables (means, SDs, speedups) across tasks.
2. Convergence plots with AUBC shaded by variance.
3. Scatter of GP condition-number vs. wall-clock (illustrates stability–speed linkage).
4. Qualitative case study: show two contrasted curves where sigmoid-BOIL failed due to poor (m,g) guess but AAUC-BOIL succeeded.
5. Ablation heat-map: performance vs. α and truncation level to visualise insensitivity.

Implementation Considerations
• Single PyTorch + BoTorch code-base with pluggable curve-compression function.
• Lightweight logging (jsonl) to keep RAM <500 MB.
• GPU monitoring via nvidia-smi pmon; CPU time via time.perf_counter(); RAM via psutil.

By adhering to this unified strategy every experiment will interrogate the same four hypothesis pillars under identical measurement regimes, enabling sound, task-agnostic evidence that AAUC-BOIL delivers practical and theoretical advantages over existing curve-compression approaches.
